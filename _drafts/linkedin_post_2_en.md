After more than five years locked inside Team Break, I finally cracked the exit code. 🔓
(Spoiler: it wasn't 1234.)

Quick recap of the game 👇

🍩 2021 — I join as a contractor to build a quiz show where you play behind desks called Pistachio, Blueberry or Caramel. Welcome to Donut Quiz. Little did I know I'd one day be debugging a silent buzzer on site.

🦸 2022 — Round two with Heroes Academy: a floor that lights up, basketball hoops, physical challenges. This time, the software does the sweating.

✍️ March 2023 — I sign. The contractor becomes an employee.

⚙️ 2023 — I build GameEngine, our first escape-game engine. It works. Mostly, it teaches me everything not to do. Thanks for everything, we won't meet again.

🎼 2024 — Clean slate: Maestro is born. A dozen games and nine sites later, it runs zombies, dinosaurs, pirates, wizards and a pharaoh. Sometimes all at once.

🍫 La Chocolaterie: five rooms, sixteen Raspberry Pis, a Guitar Hero inside an elevator and a "marriage proposal mode". Yes, we really wrote code for that.

🏝️ 2026 — Archipel: at Aéroville, a kiosk hands you a secret word to launch your mission. It doesn't get more escape-game than that.

🤖 2026 — AI joins the game. I write the issue, an agent writes the code, I review. 200+ pull requests later… I'm still reviewing.

What's in my backpack 🎒
✅ A scenario should read like a story, not a list of IDs.
✅ The field is always right. Especially during a power cut.
✅ The best project is the one that keeps running after you leave.

Thank you Team Break for the trust, thank you to the game masters who lived through the early versions (and the bugs), thank you to my colleagues. The game goes on without me, and that's exactly how it should be.

Next room? Still looking for the clue. Hints welcome in my DMs 😉
